**[00:00]**

**Sophie:** Nina, Jonas — good to speak again. And Markus — I wasn't expecting you on this call but I'm really glad you're here.

**Markus:** Nina told me the technical session went reasonably well and I wanted to form my own view before you submit a proposal. I hope that's okay.

**Sophie:** More than okay. What would be most useful for you?

**Markus:** Let's start with what Jonas thought of the session, and then I have some questions of my own.

**Jonas:** (measured tone) The platform is capable. The ad server workflow is cleaner than what we have with Xandr — I'll give you that. The dynamic creative setup is genuinely better. My concern is the interface. My team is used to Xandr's UI. It's not perfect but it's familiar. The AdCorn interface is going to need training and there's going to be a period where my team is slower than they are today. In a busy period that's a real cost.

**Sophie:** That's a fair concern. How long does that typically take in your experience with platform transitions?

**Jonas:** Last time we moved — that was years ago — it was probably two months before people were fully up to speed. And that was with dedicated training. What does your onboarding look like?

**Sophie:** We assign a dedicated onboarding specialist for the migration period — typically twelve weeks. They're in the platform with your team, not just running webinars. We'd also configure the interface to match your most common workflows before you go live, so the things your traders do most often are front-and-centre.

**Jonas:** Who is the onboarding specialist? Someone specific, or whoever is available?

**Sophie:** A named individual who stays with you through the full migration and is still your primary contact for the first three months of live operation. I can give you the name and background of who we'd assign if that would help.

**Jonas:** Yes, I'd want that. (to Markus) The platform itself I'm okay with. The process around migration is what I need to see more detail on.

**Markus:** Fine. (to Sophie) I'm going to be direct with you. I've spoken to two people in the industry about AdCorn in the last two weeks. One runs a Berlin-based agency, similar scale to us. He used AdCorn two years ago for about six months and moved back to Xandr. He said the platform was good but the account team turned over twice in that period and the service dropped off significantly. The second person — a publisher contact — said AdCorn's buying quality has improved recently but that there were persistent brand safety issues twelve to eighteen months ago. I want to know what's changed.

**Sophie:** (pause) I appreciate you being direct. On the account team turnover — we went through a difficult period about eighteen months ago where we lost experienced account managers. That was disruptive and I won't defend it. Since then we've restructured the team, introduced minimum tenure requirements for accounts above a certain size, and the churn rate has dropped significantly. For an account of your size you would be on our senior account management tier. I can show you the team structure and the tenure of the people who'd be working with you.

**Markus:** And the brand safety point?

**Sophie:** We had gaps in our pre-bid brand safety filtering before we integrated with DoubleVerify in Q3 last year. Since that integration went live, brand safety incidents have dropped substantially. We can share reporting on that but I'd want to do it through a proper briefing rather than verbally on this call — the detail matters.

**Markus:** (not fully satisfied) Here's my problem, Sophie. I'm switching vendors because Xandr's service deteriorated. If I switch to AdCorn and the same thing happens — account team turnover, service gaps — I've traded one problem for another and I've wasted six months and a lot of my team's time. What guarantee do I have that that doesn't happen?

**Sophie:** No vendor can give you a guarantee in the contractual sense. What I can offer is: service level commitments written into the contract with financial consequences if we miss them, a named account team with tenure commitments, and access to our Head of Client Services for escalation at any time — not a ticketing system. I won't pretend the last eighteen months were smooth, but I can show you what we've done since and let you judge whether it's credible.

**Markus:** (to Nina) What's your read?

**Nina:** I think the platform is right for us. The account management history is a concern but what Sophie just described is a reasonable mitigation if it goes into the contract. My bigger concern is whether the proposal is going to be competitive on commercial terms.

**Markus:** (to Sophie) On commercial terms — what are you going to bring us?

**Sophie:** I'd want to bring you a proper proposal rather than numbers on this call, but I can tell you the structure: platform fee as a percentage of media spend, itemised clearly. Minimum commitment over twelve months. Migration support included in the first contract year. Nothing buried.

**Markus:** What percentage?

**Sophie:** I need to base it on your confirmed media volume. If you can give me a media budget assumption for the first twelve months I can come back with a specific number.

**Markus:** We'd be migrating roughly sixty million of our eighty-five into the platform initially. The rest is on channels we're not moving yet.

**Sophie:** That's helpful. I'll build the proposal around sixty million.

**Markus:** One more thing. Your proposal needs to include a break clause. If the service level commitments aren't being met at the six-month mark, I want the right to exit without penalty. I'm not signing a twelve-month contract with no way out if things go wrong again.

**Sophie:** I'd need to confirm that with our commercial team — it's not a standard term — but I'll raise it. I want to be transparent: there may be a version of a break clause we can accommodate and a version we can't. I'll come back with what's possible.

**Markus:** Fair enough. Nina, anything else?

**Nina:** Close date — Sophie, when are you submitting the proposal?

**Sophie:** Twenty-fifth of June.

**Nina:** We need it by then. We're doing vendor comparisons in early July and I want to give you a fair shot, but the timeline is tight.

**Markus:** (standing to leave) Sophie — I'm going to be honest. You're not the obvious choice. But Nina thinks the platform is right and I trust her judgement technically. What's going to matter to me is the commercial terms and whether I believe the service story. Make sure the proposal addresses both. (disconnects)

**Nina:** (quietly) That went okay. He was harder on the other vendors.

**Sophie:** Good to know. Jonas — anything else you need before the proposal?

**Jonas:** Named onboarding specialist. Migration timeline at our volume. And SLA detail — what's your response time commitment for a P1 incident?

**Sophie:** I'll include all of that. Thank you both.
