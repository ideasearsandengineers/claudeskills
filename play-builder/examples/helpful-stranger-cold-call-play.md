# Example play: The Helpful-Stranger Cold Call

> Built by Play Builder v0.1 from a real seller's approach, told as a rough story. Seller anonymised. One tactic changed for honesty (see Checks).

**Source story (rough, as told):** "A seller in Denmark wasn't afraid to cold call. All the other sellers seemed allergic to the phone, and it isn't a typical tactic in ad tech sales. His approach built pipeline, and he ran a training session to share it. Practices like acting as a 'confused old man', for example mispronouncing their name and getting them to correct you, instantly form a connection where they're helping you. Then they help you understand their problem, and they connect the dots to your solution."

**Checks:** ✓ Play, not strategy. ✓ Repeatable: any seller can learn it with practice. ⚠ **Honesty change:** the original tactic involved pretending to be confused and deliberately mispronouncing names. That can come across as manipulative or disrespectful, because names matter to people. The honest principle behind it (people warm up when they help you) is kept, using genuine requests for help instead. ✓ Evidence: built pipeline in one market and was taught to others [Given]; results to confirm.
**Note on rules:** cold calling rules differ by country (for example, registration lists and consent rules). Check local rules before running this play.

---

**PLAY: The Helpful-Stranger Cold Call**
*Open a cold call by genuinely asking for help, so the prospect starts as a helper, not a target.*

**Use it when (trigger):** you need new pipeline in a market where few competitors use the phone, and you have a named prospect but no warm introduction.
**Don't use it when:** the prospect has asked not to be called, local rules restrict calls, or you haven't done basic research on them.
**Goal:** the prospect explains a problem in their own words and agrees to a short follow-up.
**Who's involved:** the seller; the prospect (ideally the person who owns the problem).

**Steps**
1. **Prepare:** research the person and company for five minutes; have one relevant insight ready. Check local calling rules.
2. **Open with a genuine request for help:** check you have the right person, or how to say their name correctly. People instinctively help.
3. **The core move:** ask for their help understanding something: "I'm trying to understand how teams like yours handle X."
4. **Listen and let them connect:** ask follow-ups until they describe a problem; then link it briefly to what you do.
5. **Next step:** ask for a short meeting to continue, at a time that suits them.

**Talk track**
- "Hi, is this [name]? I want to make sure I'm saying your name correctly."
- "I wonder if you could help me. I'm trying to understand how brands like yours handle [problem]. Is that something your team deals with?"
- "That's really helpful. How does that affect [their goal]?"
- "Would it be worth 20 minutes next week to see whether we could help with that?"

**Discovery questions**
- "What have you tried so far?"
- "Who else is involved in that?"
- "What would make it worth fixing this year?"

**Likely objections and honest answers**
- *"I'm busy."* "Of course. Is there a better time for two minutes, or shall I email one question instead?"
- *"Just send me an email."* "Happy to. What's the one thing that would make it worth reading?"

**Proof:** a seller in Denmark built pipeline with this approach where colleagues didn't use the phone, then trained the team on it [Given, anonymised; figures to confirm].
**Why it works:** asking for help triggers reciprocity and lowers defences. People feel good when they help, and they explain their problems more openly to someone who is listening than to someone who is pitching.
**Common mistakes:** pitching in the first 30 seconds; asking for help with something trivial or fake; talking more than the prospect; giving up after one call.

**How you'll know it's working**
- **Leading signal:** the prospect explains a problem in their own words during the call.
- **Outcome signal:** meetings booked per hour of calling; pipeline created from calls.
- **What to track:** log calls and meetings with a "cold call" source in the CRM, and compare pipeline per seller with sellers who don't call [To confirm: CRM field].

**Coaching tip:** pair new callers with the best caller for one hour a week: listen live, then swap. Build confidence by starting with friendly, lower-stakes prospects.

**Source:** one real seller's approach in ad tech, taught to the wider team [Given]; evidence strength: a pattern in one market, to be validated elsewhere.
