# Example play: The Expert Peer Advisor

> Built by Play Builder v0.1 from a real account story, told as a rough story. Client and colleagues anonymised.

**Source story (rough, as told):** "I went into a UK betting company to help the local team. Their leader was always very quiet. I used my background to ask deep questions about how his data team justified their ROI, and he began telling me they were experimenting with attribution modelling. I asked questions that drew on my deep technical knowledge of the topic, and the leader wanted me to come back every week just to speak to their team and guide them. The client started spending a lot more after that."

**Checks:** ✓ Play, not strategy. ⚠ Repeatable with support: it depends on real expertise in the buyer's topic, so most sellers will run it by bringing a specialist, and the specialist must have time for follow-up sessions. ✓ Honest, provided the advice is genuinely useful and not a disguised pitch. ⚠ Evidence: one strong story [Given]; validate with more examples.

**PLAY: The Expert Peer Advisor**
*Win a quiet, technical buyer by helping their team with the problem they're working on, as a peer, not a vendor.*

**Use it when (trigger):** a senior buyer is quiet or hard to read in meetings, leads a technical or analytical team, and is under pressure to prove the return on their spend.
**Don't use it when:** the buyer wants a quick commercial answer, or you can't bring someone with genuine depth in their topic. Shallow expertise is quickly exposed and damages trust.
**Goal:** the buyer invites you back to work with their team, and your product becomes part of how they solve the problem.
**Who's involved:** the seller; a specialist with deep knowledge of the buyer's problem; the buyer and their analysts or data team.

**Steps**
1. **Prepare:** learn how this type of team usually measures its return, and the methods they may be trying (for example, attribution modelling). Bring the specialist.
2. **Open:** ask about *their* work, not your product: how do they prove the value of their spend today?
3. **The core move:** when they mention something they're experimenting with, go deep. Ask expert questions about method, data and pitfalls. Show that you understand their world.
4. **Give before you ask:** share useful advice, even if it doesn't involve your product.
5. **Next step:** offer a recurring working session with their team, with a clear purpose, such as reviewing their model together.

**Talk track**
- "How does your team show the board that this spend is working?"
- "What are you experimenting with at the moment to measure it better?"
- "How are you handling [a known pitfall in their method]? That's where most teams get stuck."
- "Would it help if I came in with your team for an hour each week while you build this?"

**Discovery questions**
- "What would 'good' look like for your model in six months?"
- "Which decisions will it change once you trust it?"
- "Where does your data come from today, and where are the gaps?"

**Likely objections and honest answers**
- *"Why would you help us for free?"* "Because if your measurement improves, you'll invest with more confidence, and we'd like to earn a share of that."
- *"We have consultants for this."* "Great. I can work alongside them on the parts closest to our platform."

**Proof:** a quiet senior buyer at a UK betting company invited weekly sessions with their team, and spend grew significantly afterwards [Given, anonymised; figures to confirm].
**Why it works:** quiet technical leaders trust demonstrated competence, not enthusiasm. Helping with their real problem makes you part of their team, and buyers spend more with people who make them look good internally.
**Common mistakes:** steering every answer back to the product; bringing someone without real depth; offering help that isn't followed through; not linking the sessions to a business outcome.

**How you'll know it's working**
- **Leading signal:** the buyer talks more, brings their team in, and invites you back.
- **Outcome signal:** spend increases, or a new use case for your product appears.
- **What to track:** tag accounts where the play was used, and compare spend growth with similar accounts [To confirm: CRM field].

**Coaching tip:** before each meeting, the specialist and seller agree the three expert questions they'll ask, and the one piece of useful advice they'll give, whether or not it involves the product.
