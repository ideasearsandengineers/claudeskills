# Example play: The Hidden Use Case

> Built by Play Builder v0.1 from a real win, told as a rough story. Client and colleagues anonymised.

**Source story (rough, as told):** "They said we couldn't sell the ad server. They only wanted the DSP because they didn't understand where the ad server could add value. I turned up without a salesperson, in a relaxed setting away from the office over a morning coffee, and asked about the biggest difficulty in running a global brand with so many agencies. He said he just wanted to know the exact moment all his campaigns went live, in a way the agencies couldn't disrupt. After a brief pause I explained that the ad server could do that. It's such a simple use case most people wouldn't have thought of it. The client began asking lots of questions. A year later we signed the global ad server deal, the largest we ever signed."

**Checks:** ✓ Play, not strategy (one seller or specialist can run it). ⚠ Repeatable with support: the "connect" step needs deep product knowledge, so less experienced sellers should bring a specialist. ✓ Honest. ⚠ Evidence: one strong story [Given]; validate with two or three more examples.

---

**PLAY: The Hidden Use Case**
*Turn "we don't need that product" into a new deal by finding a simple job it already does for this buyer.*

**Use it when (trigger):** a buyer has decided they only need part of your offer, and has ruled out another product because they don't see where it fits, often at a large, complex organisation (many brands, markets or agencies).
**Don't use it when:** the buyer has a clear, evidenced reason for ruling it out (for example, a long contract with a competitor), or you're early in qualification and haven't earned the meeting.
**Goal:** the buyer names a real problem the ruled-out product solves, and asks to explore it.
**Who's involved:** a specialist or senior expert who knows the product deeply; the seller optional (their absence can lower the buyer's guard); a senior buyer who owns the business problem, not just the purchase.

**Steps**
1. **Prepare:** know the products' less obvious capabilities, and the buyer's operating complexity (markets, agencies, brands).
2. **Open:** meet informally, away from the office if you can, for example over coffee. Make it a conversation, not a pitch. Don't bring slides.
3. **The core move:** ask about the hardest part of running their business at scale, then listen. Don't mention the product.
4. **Connect:** when they describe a problem the product solves, pause, then explain simply how it would work for them, in their words.
5. **Next step:** let their questions lead. Agree a follow-up to explore the use case with the right people.

**Talk track**
- "Leaving our products aside for a moment, what's the hardest part of running a brand across this many markets and agencies?"
- "What would you most like to know that you can't see today?"
- "If you could see that in real time, what would it change for you?"
- (After a pause) "That's actually something we can do, and simply. Can I show you how it would work?"

**Discovery questions**
- "Who feels that problem most, day to day?"
- "How do you find out today, and how long does it take?"
- "What happens when it goes wrong?"

**Likely objections and honest answers**
- *"We already decided we don't need that product."* "Understood. I'm not here to change that. I'm curious how this one problem is handled today."
- *"Our agencies handle that."* "That makes sense. Do you get the view you want, or the view they give you?"

**Proof:** a global brand that ruled out the ad server signed a global ad server deal a year later, the company's largest [Given, anonymised].
**Why it works:** buyers judge products by the use cases they already know. Asking about their hardest problem, without selling, lets them discover a use case for themselves, and people believe what they discover more than what they're told.
**Common mistakes:** pitching too early; asking about "requirements" instead of their hardest problem; filling the pause; explaining the full product instead of the one use case.

**How you'll know it's working**
- **Leading signal:** the buyer starts asking questions about how it would work.
- **Outcome signal:** a follow-up meeting with new stakeholders; a new opportunity for the ruled-out product.
- **What to track:** tag opportunities where the play was used, and compare how often they convert with similar deals where it wasn't [To confirm: CRM field].

**Coaching tip:** a 10-minute role-play where the "buyer" has a hidden problem written on a card. The seller may only ask questions until the buyer names it, then has one minute to connect it simply.
